### Look, character, trust

**Fresh Clean Tees**

- **Look:** a large product photo with a tall column of choices. Plain, functional, navy on white.
- **Character:** Everyperson is a plausible reading ("A classic fit tee for the average sized guy").
- **Trust and action:** a 4.6 rating from 28,791 reviews and "369 bought in past week" are displayed social proof. I could not verify them.

**Bombas**

- **Look:** a large photo hero, then rounded color cards with heavy type. Warm and bold.
- **Character:** Caregiver is a plausible reading ("You did good… real good").
- **Trust and action:** "200 million+ donations" and 4,000+ Giving Partners are the brand's own numbers. Naming the giving steps makes the claim easier to follow, but it does not prove it.

**Love Your Melon**

- **Look:** large product photos, a hand-drawn blue logo, and rounded shapes. Soft and friendly.
- **Character:** Caregiver is a plausible reading ("warmth and comfort to kids").
- **Trust and action:** "Join the Family" reads as unity. "4 left" labels read as scarcity, but I could not verify the stock. Impact totals are the brand's own claims.

### What this adds up to

- All three show proof Jersey Givers does not have: reviews, purchase counts, or donation totals. We should not compete on proof, and must not invent any.
- None of these pages speaks to one place or to students who volunteer. That is a possible opening, not proven demand.
- **Largest gap found:** both giving brands state what a purchase does. Jersey Givers has a cause but no stated mechanism.

### How each site serves its audience (learner's analysis)

Learner's own explanations, given in answer to one AI question per site.

- **Fresh Clean Tees:** Everyperson. Serves someone worried about how the shirt will look on them: the page puts more emphasis on a good fit, with each model's height and size described. (Learner first compared this to Amazon, then corrected it: Amazon usually has size guides too.) Persuasion: social proof through customer reviews, and reciprocity through the free "Find My Size" tool and sizing guide, offered before any purchase. (Learner first suggested the pack discount as reciprocity; after the AI explained that a discount requires buying, the learner chose the sizing help instead.)

- **Bombas:** Learner's reading: Hero, for buyers who want to "leave a mark" (the AI's earlier reading was Caregiver; both are interpretations). Persuasion: liking through a shared cause, and social proof in the big numbers, which signal "they are a legitimate cause that is helping the world", giving "more reasons to contribute". Limit: the totals are the brand's own claims.

- **Love Your Melon:** The college-class origin presents it as grassroots. Learner: "Grassroots orgs receive more respect than big companies because grassroots are more likely to do a cause for the people." Persuasion: "Join the Family" appeals to unity, which "can lead their customers to trust them more." Assumption: that shoppers trust grassroots brands more is the learner's belief, not tested.

- **Jersey Givers (comparison):** Learner identifies unity ("Pull it on, join the cause"; "From Jersey, for Jersey") and reciprocity ("First shift? Do this.") on the storefront. What serves the audience better: the page "directly say[s] who I am serving" ("Jersey Givers is for students…"), and it is "less cluttered and more clean" than Fresh Clean Tees. What to borrow: Fresh Clean Tees "does better" with its sizing guide, "an extra method of reciprocity". Carry into Plan: a fit guide beside the sizes, with measurements labeled as fictional demo data.

### Audience choice

**Position A, the crew shirt** (learner's choice, Nov 9, after an AI recommendation of A). Learner's reason: "People who are most likely to want the shirt are driven students who want to be marketable to colleges (if they're in high school), or employers if they are in college." Newcomers are "less likely to commit", so they stay the secondary visitor rather than the main audience.

- **Assumption to check:** that wanting to look good to colleges or employers drives the purchase. Not tested.
- **New idea:** this would include high school students, which is wider than the current college-student persona. Revisit in Plan.

### Honest persuasion carried forward

- **Unity:** "From Jersey, for Jersey." Limit: an intended connection, with no measured effect.
- **Reciprocity:** the "First shift? Do this." tip. Limit: useful advice does not prove more purchases.
